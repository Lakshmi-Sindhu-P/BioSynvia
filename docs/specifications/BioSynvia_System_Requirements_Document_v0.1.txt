Problem statement and intended solution
The problem. Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell-culture experiments produce frequent bioreactor sensor
measurements, but biologically important attributes may be available only at intermittent offline assay times. A model
might estimate the missing state between assays, but apparent performance can be inflated by future-data leakage,
data from related reactors appearing in both training and testing, or evaluation based on interpolated labels rather
than actual assays.

System objective. BioSynvia (working title) shall provide a reproducible, offline research pipeline that determines
whether available inline sensor observations can estimate a verified, measured biological target in unseen CHO
cultivation experiments. It shall compare simple/statistical/ML methods under group-safe validation, disclose errors
and uncertainties, and present results with transparent provenance through a read-only dashboard. A
mechanistic/hybrid extension is conditional on experimental data suitability.

Expected result. A traceable scientific research prototype: source manifest, vetted target and units, canonical records,
frozen experiment-group splits, reproducible baselines and candidate models, per-experiment metrics, failure
analysis, model card, read-only user interface and independently repeatable demonstration. It is not a
manufacturing control application, a qualified soft sensor, a production Digital Twin, or a clinically validated
product.

1. Purpose, scope and system boundaries
1.1 Purpose and intended readers

This SRD translates BioSynvia’s scientific/product objectives into implementable and testable system behavior. It is
intended for the developer, scientific reviewer, QA reviewer, and a hiring manager evaluating scientific rigor. It
establishes required artifacts, workflows, interfaces, invariants, acceptance tests and release restrictions; detailed
implementation choices remain subject to controlled technical-design decisions.

1.2 In scope for the first presentable research version (V1)
   •        Offline ingestion of a legally obtained CHO source archive with provenance and integrity verification.
   •        Experiment/reactor inventory; original/aligned time, units, missingness and data-quality audits.
   •        Recorded selection of a real offline assay target based on coverage and scientific meaning.
   •        A typed canonical representation of experiments, paired reactors, sensors and assays.

   •        Sensor-only inference: at time t, use only validated inline data available at or before t.
   •        Experiment-group partitioning, versioned/frozen manifests and training-only preprocessing.
   •        Simple baselines, Ridge and/or PLS, and one supported nonlinear candidate; group-aware model comparison.
   •        Per-experiment model performance, missingness/abstention, negative findings and limitations.
   •        Versioned artifact and evidence manifests, tests, locally reproducible commands and read-only dashboard.

1.3 Conditional advanced extension (V1.1/P2)

Mechanistic ODEs, identifiable parameter estimation, mechanistic–statistical hybrid correction and hypothetical
scenarios are out of V1’s required scope. They become data-calibrated claims only after PRD gate G7/MH-01 confirms
observed states, correct units, appropriate feeds/volumes and identifiability. Otherwise they may be explored only as
explicitly synthetic, educational simulations.

1.4 Out of scope and prohibited interpretations

No live manufacturing connection; no automatic control action; no patient/clinical decision; no release-quality,
GMP/GxP or FDA-compliant validation claim; no real-time/continuous production telemetry; no commercial product

1.5 Operational assumptions and unresolved questions
   •         SOURCE: the publisher describes 24 CHO-K1 cultivations from 12 paired experiments (9 batch, 3 fed-batch),
             38 inline and 10 offline variables, and original Excel plus aligned CSV data [R1].
   •         NOT YET VERIFIED BY IMPLEMENTATION: precise CSV header names, observation units, target assay
             name, source license, feature availability, viable cell density coverage, timestamps, measured limits, device
             calibration and realistic prediction latency. These must be resolved through Phase P1.
   •         GATED: the 2-experiment final holdout is only a candidate protocol; if the data do not permit a defensible
             holdout, grouped leave-one-experiment-out with nested tuning must be reviewed and documented instead.
   •         GATED: business/scientific utility error thresholds cannot be invented. A scientist must select any target-
             specific acceptance threshold after unit and use-case review, before a utility claim.

Architecture decision (PROPOSED): use a Python package (src/biosynvia), local filesystem artifacts
(CSV/Parquet/JSON/YAML), scikit-learn/SciPy, pytest, and Streamlit. This stack is not a requirement to deploy cloud
infrastructure or a database. A SQLite experiment index may be considered later, but the source of truth in V1 should
remain immutable manifests and reproducible files.

6.4 Source identifier and unit policy

All source fields receive stable IDs from field_dictionary.csv, distinct from their display names. Units are never
guessed from names such as X, VCD, CO2 or feed. Conversion requires a written transformation, conversion
constant, dimensional compatibility review, and test fixture. A normalized value must preserve a pointer to its raw
value and original unit. If a scientifically required variable’s unit is unknown, any data-calibrated mechanistic
component using it is blocked.

Missingness semantics: distinguish not_sampled, sensor_unavailable, parse_invalid,
below_detection_limit, late_report, unit_unknown and excluded_by_protocol, to the extent the source
supports them. If the source cannot distinguish these categories, retain unknown_missing_cause rather than
inventing a reason.

7.2 API and user interface boundaries

The V1 dashboard reads a report manifest, tables, and precomputed approved predictions from local files. It must not
call fit(), perform auto-tuning, modify raw data, rewrite artifacts, or calculate hidden new model outputs without
making the computation/revision explicit. It should not collect or send viewer data to third-party analytics. Public
HTTP access is not required and should remain disabled by default.

The UI information architecture comprises Overview, Data Explorer, Model Comparison, Prediction Review, and
Quality & Provenance. An optional Mechanistic Research tab is hidden or marked experimental until gate G7
passes. Every prediction view includes a plain-text target unit, measured-versus-predicted distinction, applicability
status, model version and source experiment details.

8. Scientific model and validation specification
8.1 Inference contract and sample construction

V1 shall estimate a measured offline target at a measured offline time using only inline sensors that would have
been available by that cutoff. It shall not use prior offline targets in this primary sensor-only track. If a separate lab-
assisted track is later designed, the most recent offline target is eligible only when its report availability time, not just
collection time, precedes the cutoff; the track must be evaluated and reported separately.

Modeling samples shall be constructed by linking one real offline assay with one point-in-time feature snapshot, or an
explicit unscorable/abstained status. There shall be no pseudo-sample inflation from interpolation of target values.
Cross-sectional sample weights must not make long cultivations dominate independent-experiment generalization
claims.

8.2 Split and cross-validation specification

Partition by experiment_id so both parallel reactors remain together. The default proposed final test is two
experiment groups, ideally representing batch and fed-batch if coverage and scientific design permit. This is a
candidate protocol, not a predetermined assignment; frozen IDs and stratification decisions must be recorded
before model tuning. Development hyperparameters are selected only through experiment-group-aware folds. Since
fed-batch has three experiments, confidence about cross-mode generalization will be limited even under a balanced
holdout.

If adequate target coverage across independent groups is lacking, the team shall reassess the task and possibly prefer
grouped leave-one-experiment-out evaluation with nested tuning and no exaggerated independent-test claim. The
report must make clear which protocol was actually executed.

8.3 Model families and fair comparison
   •       Naive: training-group mean/median or a declared operationally available sensor-only proxy. A last-known
           offline target baseline is not an equal sensor-only baseline unless explicitly designated lab-assisted.
   •       Transparent statistical: calibrated single-sensor relationship; Ridge/Elastic Net; partial least squares
           regression (PLS) for correlated sensor families.
   •       Nonlinear: Random Forest or histogram-based gradient boosting, subject to group-count and feature-
           dimension constraints.
   •       Optional uncertainty: Gaussian process or group-aware prediction interval method only with documented
           assumptions and appropriate evaluation coverage.
   •       P2 mechanistic: transparent ordinary differential equations with units, initial states, parameter ranges,
           solver verification and identifiability analysis. No data-backed calibration is claimed when measured
           states/feeds do not support it.
   •       P2 hybrid: mechanistic baseline plus train-only statistical residual model, evaluated against both components
           under identical grouped folds.

Model families should receive comparable allowed inputs, scoring rows, fold assignments and preprocessing policies.
When sample availability differs, show the difference instead of silently using disjoint scoring sets and comparing
errors as if they were equivalent.

8.4 Metrics and reporting

The proposed primary ranking is macro-averaged experiment-level MAE in the original target unit. For each
experiment compute MAE, RMSE, signed bias, valid scored assay count, abstention count and baseline-relative delta.
Secondary summaries include median/worst-group error and process-mode stratification. R-squared is optional and
must be interpreted carefully for low-variance per-run targets.

Do not invent a scientific acceptance threshold. After the target and units are established, solicit scientific review to
define meaningful error tolerance. A model may be declared best among compared methods, not automatically fit
for operational use.

Uncertainty estimates must be marked as calibrated or uncalibrated, with empirical coverage and interval width
where estimable. Repeated timepoints and paired reactors cannot be treated as fully independent observations for
confidence intervals. If calibration data are too limited, publish descriptive variability and abstain from coverage
guarantees.

8.5 Explainability, failure and robustness

Evaluate at least one sensor-family ablation (e.g., turbidity/permittivity versus gas signals when corresponding fields
exist), a missing-sensor stress test, a batch/fed-batch comparison, and a per-experiment error table. Feature
attribution should use approaches appropriate for correlated signals and should not claim causal biochemical
relevance. An explanation is a description of model behavior, not a demonstrated biological mechanism.

Preserve negative results, instability, out-of-distribution warnings and nonconverged candidate runs. A candidate that
fails on fed-batch experiments cannot be called broadly generalizable simply because overall pooled RMSE appears
low.

8.6 Optional mechanistic feasibility gate

The mechanistic extension shall not claim a calibrated biological state model unless data audit confirms required
concentrations, volumes/feed schedules, known units, time origins, plausible assumptions and sufficient independent
evidence for identifiability. Sensitivity/parameter-correlation or multi-start analyses are mandatory before
interpreting fitted parameters. Otherwise its solver may remain a synthetic example outside the main empirical
evaluation, clearly described as illustrative.

not equivalent to trademark clearance.

11.2 Milestone acceptance criteria
     •   M1 Data-ready: DOI/checksum, experiment/reactor structure, target and units verified; documented go/no-go.
     •   M2 Model-ready: group splits frozen, sensor-only inference contract, future-perturbation and preprocessing
         isolation tests pass, valid baseline established.
     •   M3 Research-ready: immutable first holdout run, per-experiment metric table, failure analysis, limitations
         and model card; clean reproduction passes.
     •   M4 Demo-ready: UI shows measured/predicted/missing states and traceable model version; no
         production/GxP/CGT claims; publication gates reviewed.
     •   M5 Advanced-ready (optional): mechanistic states/units identifiable or synthetic-only; numerical solver tests
         and same-split comparisons completed.

dashboard and 6-slide scientific case study.

Interview/portfolio language permitted only after reproduction: “Developed a research prototype for CHO cell-
culture soft sensing; compared baseline, linear/chemometric and nonlinear models using experiment-group-held-out
validation, documented uncertainty and failure cases, and created a traceable scientist-facing review interface.”
Specific accomplishments, technologies, metrics and improvements must match actual observed outputs; do not add
the prototype as completed work before implementation.


14. Source register and document precedence
The source-of-truth hierarchy is: (1) actual rights-cleared publisher dataset and verified metadata for empirical facts,
(2) BioSynvia PRD v1.1 for product scope, research intent and decision gates, (3) this SRD for proposed
implementation contracts, (4) later approved implementation designs and tested evidence for actual system behavior.
Nothing in this SRD overwrites the existing PRD.

   •     [R1] Primary dataset: Uhlendorff, S., Fulek, R., Eimler, J., Pein-Hackelbusch, M. and Frahm, B. (2026). Dataset
         Based on Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) Cultivations including Turbidity, Permittivity, O2 and CO2
         Measurements. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20829178. Dataset release record indicates 24 cultivations, 12 paired
         experiments, 38 inline and 10 offline variables; field-level contents require ingestion audit.
   •     [R2] Product specification: BioSynvia PRD v1.1 — Problem Statement First, November 4, 2026. Available
         unchanged as the user-approved product source.
   •     [R3] Scientific methodology: scikit-learn LeaveOneGroupOut documentation and PRD §7 experiment-design
         rules.
   •     [R4] Research and legal context: USPTO Likelihood of confusion guidance; the scientific-software-related
         SYNVIA name conflict remains unresolved.
   •     [R5] Role alignment context: User-provided July 2026 J&J Senior Scientist/Modeler job description, used for
         portfolio relevance only; no claim of employment, sponsorship approval, or still-open requisition.
